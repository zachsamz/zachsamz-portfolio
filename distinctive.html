Ministry Distinctive

Part One — Degree Definition

Master of Arts in Christian Education

The Master of Arts in Christian Education at Dallas Theological Seminary prepares students for teaching, leadership, and discipleship roles in local church and parachurch ministry contexts. The program integrates biblical and theological foundations with practical ministry formation, equipping graduates to design and implement discipleship strategies, lead with theological integrity, communicate the gospel across generations and cultures, and develop the spiritual and educational structures that form Christlike communities. The degree assumes that Christian education is not merely the transmission of information but the formation of persons — the work of the Holy Spirit, through Scripture and community, conforming believers to the image of Christ.

Part Two — Reflections

Reflective Statements

Overall Reflective Statement

For my competence in the Master of Arts in Christian Education degree, I am submitting my ST5104 Doctrinal Synthesis Paper on soteriology and my ST5102 Doctrinal Statement on Trinitarianism, which together demonstrate what I have learned, integrated, and been transformed by in the theological core of the program. The soteriology paper represents my most developed theological writing to date — a full treatment of the doctrine of salvation that moves from the technical (the ordo salutis, the extent of the atonement, the nature of saving faith) to the pastoral (how soteriology shapes evangelism, counseling, discipleship, and leadership). The Trinitarian doctrinal statement represents the same pattern: rigorous theological exposition applied directly to pastoral ministry. Both papers were written not as academic exercises but as theological formation documents — genuine wrestling with what I believe about God and why it matters for the people I serve.

Learning Reflective Statement

As I developed in the MACE degree and the ministry experiences of this residency year, I learned that theology is not what you know about God — it is what knowing God has done to you. I came into the program with a theological framework that was broadly Reformed and evangelical, shaped by years of reading MacArthur, Calvin, and the Puritans. What the coursework did was not replace that framework but deepen it, sharpen it, and force me to defend it at a level of precision I had not previously required of myself. The soteriology paper, in particular, reshaped how I think about pastoral counseling. When I understand that regeneration precedes faith — that the spiritually dead sinner cannot choose God without prior divine action — I approach counseling conversations differently. I am not trying to motivate someone toward a decision; I am praying for God to open a heart that cannot open itself. That is not a small shift. It changes the posture of the whole conversation. The Trinity paper shaped my preaching. The doctrine of the Trinity is not a theological puzzle to be solved; it is the revealed character of the God who loves us. Preaching the Trinity means preaching a relational, pursuing, intimately present God — not an abstraction.

Lifelong Development Statement

As a result of the experiences documented by these artifacts, I will continue to develop in the MACE degree by treating the theological disciplines not as completed coursework but as permanent pastoral resources. The doctrines of soteriology and Trinitarianism are not subjects I have finished; they are wells I will draw from for the rest of my ministry. I intend to continue reading in systematic theology annually, not to accumulate academic knowledge but to deepen the theological reserves from which I preach, counsel, and lead. I also intend to do more work at the intersection of theology and pastoral practice — the kind of work modeled in the practical implications sections of both papers. The gap between what pastors believe theologically and how they function pastorally is one of the most significant problems in evangelical ministry today. I want to close that gap, not in others first, but in myself. Theology that does not form the pastor cannot form the congregation. The work of integration is lifelong, and I am committed to it.
