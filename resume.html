Ministry Perspective

Philosophy, Passion & Preparation

I believe the local church is the hope of the world — not because of its programs or its platform, but because of the gospel it proclaims and the Christ it exalts. My philosophy of ministry flows from a conviction that the Word of God, faithfully proclaimed and practically applied, is sufficient to transform individuals, families, and entire communities. The pastoral office is not a platform for personal visibility; it is a stewardship before God on behalf of His people.

Practically, I am committed to expository preaching that takes the text seriously and trusts the Holy Spirit with the results; relational discipleship that forms people in authentic community; elder-led church governance that is accountable and servant-hearted; and the intentional development of the next generation of leaders who will outlast me in ministry. I hold to biblical inerrancy, cessationism, complementarianism, and credobaptism. I preach in the Reformed tradition and draw deeply from Calvin, Owen, Watson, Lloyd-Jones, and MacArthur, while remaining committed to accessibility and clarity for ordinary people — because I am one.

Conversion

How I Got Here

I came to faith at twenty-three years old, after completing military service, through the patient faithfulness of a believer who refused to stop pursuing me with the gospel. Every Sunday morning when I stand before our congregation, I am aware that someone in the room is exactly where I once was — and that awareness drives everything about how I preach and how I pastor.

My passion is for the unglamorous, sustained, ordinary work of ministry: preaching the text faithfully week after week, sitting with people in crisis, investing in leaders who will outlast me, and building a campus culture where the love of Christ is visible and the Word of God is central. I am particularly burdened for men — men who need to be discipled, challenged, and sent. The Stand Firm men’s ministry and my smaller discipleship cohorts reflect that burden directly.
